\title{Reference-Anchored Latent Space Projection for\\
Isolating Disease Signals in Single-Cell Data}
\author{Vladimir Opait \\

\begin{abstract}
Reference mapping places a new single-cell cohort into the coordinate
system of an existing atlas, but technical batch effects between the two
can either obscure or mimic biological signal. Existing transfer-learning
methods such as scArches extend a frozen reference with batch-specific
adaptor weights and leave the reference latents unchanged; the adaptation
of query cells is then governed only by the ELBO and by implicit
regularization of the adaptor. We present scGRIP, which makes the
geometric constraint on the query explicit. A single expression-facing
linear layer of a frozen scVI encoder is updated under three disjoint
objectives: a healthy ELBO on query cells, a functional anchor to the
frozen reference geometry, and a stratified MMD term that aligns healthy
query cells to an independent reference bank. Healthy donors are split
into an MMD-seen group~A and a held-out group~B, and disease cells are
never used in any gradient. scGRIP rests on the same assumptions about a
correct latent space that underlie MapQC. This draft covers phase~I only;
a disease-aware phase~II is a separate protocol.
\end{abstract}


\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Large-scale reference atlases such as the Human Lung Cell Atlas and the
CZ CELLxGENE Census now provide integrated representations of healthy
cell states across tissues and individuals~\cite{cellxgene_census_2023}.
A central use of these atlases is \emph{reference mapping}: projecting a
new query cohort onto an existing reference to place it in a shared
coordinate system for downstream comparison. New cohorts, however, are
generated with different chemistry, depth, and study-specific handling,
so a naive projection is confounded by technical batch effects that can
either obscure or mimic biological signal.

Existing transfer-learning methods such as scArches extend a frozen
reference with new batch-specific adaptor weights and, by construction,
leave the reference latent coordinates
unchanged~\cite{scarches_2022}. What they do not provide is an explicit
term that constrains the adapted query geometry to remain consistent
with the reference, nor a mechanism that separates healthy alignment
from disease-specific signal; adaptation is driven by the ELBO and by
whatever regularization the adaptor imposes on itself.

We present scGRIP, a reference-mapping method for a frozen scVI
model~\cite{scvi_2018} that makes this constraint explicit. Rather than
adding new adaptor capacity, scGRIP updates a single expression-facing
linear layer under three disjoint objectives: a healthy ELBO on query
cells, a functional anchor that ties the adapted latent back to the
frozen reference geometry, and a stratified maximum mean discrepancy
(MMD) term that aligns healthy query cells to an independent reference
bank. Healthy donors are partitioned into an MMD-seen group~A and a
held-out group~B, and disease cells are never used in any gradient.
scGRIP is built on the same assumptions about a correct latent space
that underlie MapQC~\cite{mapqc_2025}: that a healthy reference captures
most healthy variation, and that disease is transcriptionally distinct
from healthy. The method is illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All experiments reported here use phase~I training only: the adapter is
fit on healthy query cells, and disease cells never enter any gradient
term. Every run below was performed on the frozen 3000-gene Census base
model with the same reference bank and the same GSE298578 query cohort
(Section~\ref{sec:methods}).


\subsection{Full combinatorial screen: 10/10 leave-two-donors-out pairs}

The query cohort contains five healthy donors (HD\_1--HD\_5). To obtain a
complete rather than sampled evaluation, we enumerated all
\(\binom{5}{2}=10\) ways to hold out two donors as group~B (the MMD-unseen
split) while the remaining three form group~A (the MMD source). Every pair
was trained and evaluated independently. This is a full combinatorial
screen, not a random subset of seeds.

Across all 10 pairs, the core-stratum distance in the held-out B cells
(CM~CD4 + EM~CD4) decreases monotonically with training
(Table~\ref{tab:main-10pairs}). At epoch~10 the median improvement is
\(-0.850\), with the full range \([-1.073, -0.703]\). All 10 of 10 pairs
give a negative \(\Delta\), so the effect is not driven by a favorable
split.

\subsection{MMD is the dominant driver of alignment}

To separate the contribution of the MMD term from that of the healthy
ELBO and the anchor, we re-ran the identical B-pairs with
\(\lambda_{\text{MMD}}=0\). At epoch~10, the mean \(\Delta\) in held-out
B drops from approximately \(-0.65\) with
\(\lambda_{\text{MMD}}=500\) to approximately \(-0.15\) with
\(\lambda_{\text{MMD}}=0\) (Table~\ref{tab:mmd-vs-nommd}). The anchor and
ELBO alone therefore produce a small but nonzero effect; MMD supplies
roughly a \(4\)--\(5\times\) larger improvement.

We note a caveat on the current numbers. The core-stratum metric
(\texttt{delta\_core\_B\_mean\_distance}) has so far been computed only
for the \(\lambda_{\text{MMD}}=500\) runs. The
\texttt{anchor100\_mmd0\_all\_pairs} runs currently report the aggregate
stratified-B metric only; recomputing the core-stratum column is a
one-command follow-up and the direction of the comparison is not expected
to change.

\subsection{Anchor budget: large drift reduction at small integration cost}

Increasing the anchor weight suppresses drift of replay latents away from
their frozen base positions, at a small cost in integration
(Table~\ref{tab:anchor-abl}). Going from
\(\lambda_{\text{anchor}}=20\) to \(\lambda_{\text{anchor}}=100\)
reduces held-out anchor drift by a factor of \(2.4\)
(\(0.121 \to 0.050\)) while the held-out B improvement changes by only
about \(3\%\) (\(-0.623 \to -0.601\)). The anchor is therefore close to
free in this regime: it buys a substantially tighter geometric
constraint on the adapted latent without materially sacrificing
alignment.

\subsection{Training length: plateau at epoch 20}

With \(\lambda_{\text{anchor}}=100\) and
\(\lambda_{\text{MMD}}=500\), the held-out B improvement grows through
the first twenty epochs and then plateaus
(Table~\ref{tab:temporal}). The optimum in this configuration is

the base model, consistent with the adapter beginning to overfit the
healthy~A cells that supply the ELBO. Selecting epoch~20 rather than
epoch~10 or epoch~50 is therefore not an arbitrary choice but follows
from the plateau.

\subsection{MMD activation threshold}

Sweeping \(\lambda_{\text{MMD}}\) at fixed
\(\lambda_{\text{anchor}}=20\), split~0, epoch~10 reveals a threshold
behavior (Table~\ref{tab:mmd-sweep}). At
\(\lambda_{\text{MMD}}\le 20\), the model does not improve held-out B and
in fact makes it slightly worse than the frozen base
(\(\Delta = +0.090\) at \(\lambda_{\text{MMD}}=0\)). Alignment becomes
beneficial at \(\lambda_{\text{MMD}}\approx 50\)--\(100\), and the effect
grows monotonically up to the largest value tested
(\(\lambda_{\text{MMD}}=1000\), \(\Delta = -0.721\)). Drift of the
replay latents grows with \(\lambda_{\text{MMD}}\), from \(3.59\) at
\(\lambda_{\text{MMD}}=5\) to \(4.90\) at \(\lambda_{\text{MMD}}=1000\);
this is the trade-off that the anchor term (Section~\ref{sec:methods})
is designed to control, and it is why the final configuration pairs a
large \(\lambda_{\text{anchor}}\) with a large
\(\lambda_{\text{MMD}}\).

\subsection{Controls: IFN-18 signature and scIB}

Two auxiliary readouts behave as expected and are reported here for
completeness rather than as selection criteria.

\textbf{IFN-18 signature.} The post-hoc Spearman correlation between the
disease IFN-18 signature and the latent representation is approximately
\(0.90\) across all \(\lambda_{\text{MMD}}\), including
\(\lambda_{\text{MMD}}=0\). The signature therefore does not distinguish
between alignment regimes. This is consistent with the design: the
signature is a positive control on the disease side, not a criterion for
choosing the healthy-alignment hyperparameters, and it is computed from
a transcriptome-wide signature cache that is independent of the 3000-gene
panel used for training.

\textbf{scIB.} The batch ASW computed against donor identity is
\texttt{NaN} in all runs, because no technical batch column was supplied
to scIB. This is a deliberate design decision: in this cohort,
\texttt{donor\_id} is treated as biological, not technical, variation,
and donor structure is part of what the method is meant to preserve.
The role of the batch-ASW diagnostic is partially covered by the
block ASW (\(0.86\)--\(0.92\)) and by \texttt{anchor\_held\_out} reported
above. The stratum ASW moves from \(0.4748\) in the base model to
approximately \(0.478\) at epoch~10: a small but consistent increase,
i.e.\ the adapter does not degrade cell-type structure and slightly
sharpens it.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

We adapt a frozen scVI reference model by updating a single
expression-facing linear layer under three disjoint objectives
(Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}). Cell populations are partitioned so
that disease cells never enter any gradient term
(Table~\ref{tab:roles}).

As the frozen reference model we use a scVI model trained on the CZ
CELLxGENE Census, which provides standardized programmatic access to
millions of annotated human cells and is widely used as a reference for
single-cell integration~\cite{cellxgene_census_2023}. The model was
trained on non-spatial human RNA sequencing data, with cells and genes
filtered based on metadata and highly variable genes selected stratified
by batch variables. We use the model as a frozen encoder: all weights,
including the decoder and the remaining encoder layers, remain at their
saved values.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Reference Mapping and Transfer Learning}

Reference mapping aims to project a new query cohort onto an existing reference atlas so that both share a common latent coordinate system. scArches implements this via architectural surgery: it freezes the reference model and adds a small number of batch-specific adaptor weights, training only those parameters on the query data~\cite{scarches_2022}. By construction, the reference latent coordinates are left unchanged. What scArches does not provide is an explicit term constraining the \emph{adapted query} geometry to remain consistent with the reference; adaptation is driven by the reconstruction objective and whatever regularization the adaptor architecture implicitly imposes.

scGRIP shares scArches' frozen-reference setting but differs in what is constrained. We do not add adaptor capacity. Instead, we update a single expression-facing linear layer under an explicit geometric anchor and a stratified MMD alignment term. The anchor penalizes drift of replay latents away from their frozen base positions, while MMD pulls healthy query cells toward an independent reference bank. Both terms act on the latent space \(z\), not on the decoder.

\subsection{trVAE: MMD for Condition Transfer}

trVAE is a conditional VAE that applies an additional MMD loss to improve merging in the latent space~\cite{trvae_2020,scarches_trvae}. However, the two methods use MMD for fundamentally different purposes, and the distinction is architectural.

In trVAE, MMD is imposed on the \emph{first decoder layer} \(y = g_1(z, s)\), not on the latent space \(z\)~\cite{trvae_2020}. The motivation is explicit: a standard CVAE may already produce a latent representation \(z\) that is approximately independent of the condition \(s\), because \(s\) is fed directly to the decoder and there is little incentive for the encoder to retain condition information~\cite{trvae_arxiv}. The problem trVAE addresses is that the \emph{decoder's} first layer \(y\) nevertheless varies strongly across conditions, producing non-compact representations that hurt out-of-sample generation. MMD on \(y\) forces these condition-specific representations into overlapping regions of support, enabling the model to learn features shared across conditions and to transform unpaired samples from one condition to another~\cite{trvae_arxiv,trvae_bioinformatics}.

The task trVAE solves is therefore \emph{condition transfer}: given a sample from condition \(s=0\), predict its counterpart under \(s=1\) (e.g., healthy \(\to\) infected, control \(\to\) treated). Disease or perturbation conditions are \emph{conditions like any other} and participate in training. The MMD term does not distinguish healthy from disease; it regularizes across all conditions to improve cross-condition prediction.

scGRIP uses MMD for a different task and at a different location. The task is \emph{reference alignment}, not condition transfer: we want healthy query cells to occupy a latent region consistent with the reference bank, while disease cells remain unconstrained by the alignment. MMD is imposed on the latent space \(z\), not on the decoder. More importantly, it is applied \emph{only} between healthy query cells (group A) and the reference metric bank. Disease cells are present in the query artifact but never enter any gradient term, as enforced by an assertion in the training code and recorded in the run manifest. There is no mechanism in trVAE that would enforce this separation, because the separation itself is not trVAE's objective.

A further distinction concerns stratification. trVAE's MMD regularization, as implemented, is computed globally or over a chosen boundary of conditions~\cite{scarches_trvae}. scGRIP computes MMD \emph{stratified by cell type}: the kernel alignment is performed within matched strata (e.g., CM CD4, EM CD4) with balanced per-stratum weighting. This choice follows from the goal: we want alignment that respects the reference's cell-type structure, not a global mixing that could conflate distinct populations.

\subsection{MapQC: Evaluation, Not Construction}

MapQC is a metric for evaluating the quality of a query-to-reference mapping~\cite{mapqc_preprint}. It assumes that a healthy reference captures most healthy variation, and that disease is transcriptionally distinct from healthy for at least some cell types. Given a mapping embedding and per-cell metadata, MapQC computes a per-cell score quantifying how far each query cell (or sample) lies from the reference relative to the expected inter-sample distances within the reference itself. Query controls are expected to have low scores; query cases with disease-specific phenotypes are expected to have higher scores.

MapQC operates \emph{after} a mapping has been produced. It does not modify the mapping, does not train a model, and does not use disease labels during any construction step. scGRIP is built on the same two assumptions about what a correct latent space looks like: that healthy reference variation is well-covered, and that disease is distinct. The difference is that scGRIP \emph{constructs} the mapping under those assumptions, through an explicit anchor and healthy-only MMD, while MapQC \emph{evaluates} whether a given mapping satisfies them. The relationship is complementary: MapQC could in principle be used to assess scGRIP outputs, and the assumptions we rely on are those that MapQC formalizes.
